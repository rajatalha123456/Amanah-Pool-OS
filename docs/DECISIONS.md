# Decision record

Where the BRD is silent, a decision was needed. Each one below was taken by the development side using its best judgement,
with the reason, how to change it, and **who still has to confirm it**. Items marked **Professional sign-off** concern tax,
Shariah or legal matters that no software team can settle: the platform is configured so that the confirmed value is a
setting, not a code change.

| # | Decision | Reason | How to change it | Confirm with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Withholding tax** is read from the jurisdiction rule pack (`wht_filer_pct`, `wht_non_filer_pct`). The seeded pack uses 15% / 30%. A payout batch is refused if the pack has no rates. | The BRD names no rate; a rate hard-coded in code would be wrong the day the law changes. | Edit the rule pack (Administration > Jurisdiction & Rule Packs). | **Professional sign-off**: a tax adviser must confirm the rates and the filer / non-filer basis before real use. |
| 2 | **Zakat is not deducted at source** by default. A pack can enable it with `zakat_deduction_pct`; the statement shows the Zakat-exempt flag and the computed figure when enabled. | The BRD asks only for "tax / Zakat support" in the investor view. Deducting at source on every payout is a Shariah and legal choice. | `zakat_deduction_pct` in the rule pack. | **Professional sign-off**: the Shariah Board decides whether and how. |
| 3 | **PER / IRR**: the appropriation rate is a percentage of profit (PER) or of the investors' share (IRR); the **cap limits the reserve balance as a percentage of pool funds**; a reserve policy above the rule pack's `per_ceiling_pct` / `irr_ceiling_pct` is refused. | The BRD lists "availability, basis, cap" as rule-pack items without values. Funds is the usual base for a balance ceiling. | Rule pack ceilings; reserve policies per pool. | Finance head and Shariah Board (the ceilings). |
| 4 | **Provisions** reduce distributable profit (BRD 7.1) once approved by a checker; write-offs use the provision first. | It is the BRD's own formula. | n/a | Finance (accounting policy). |
| 5 | **Product Manager home screen**: keeps the Command Center. | It shows pool and product counts and the manager's own queue; it carries no balances or profit. | `frontend/src/layouts/navItems.ts`. | Product owner. |
| 6 | **Who sees which screen** follows BRD section 3 (powers and restrictions) and section 13 (audiences). The BRD lists no screens per role. | A role sees what it works in and nothing it cannot use; the API enforces the same limits (browser sweep `npm run e2e:roles`). | One file: `navItems.ts` (plus the API permissions). | Product owner and each function head. |
| 7 | **Retention (proposed, not in force)**: read notifications 180 days; resolved support requests 6 years; AI recommendation records 3 years. Only read notifications are ever deleted automatically; support requests and AI records are only reported. Ledgers, statements, contracts, the audit log and Shariah records are never purged. Three drafts are seeded; one person proposes and a different person approves. | Operational data should not live forever, but evidence must. The periods are conservative defaults. | Risk proposes a new policy, an authorized person approves (Governance). | **Professional sign-off**: legal / compliance must set the legal retention periods (financial records are usually kept much longer). |
| 8 | **Settlement stays manual with the bank's own reference.** No live Raast / 1LINK / core-banking connection is built; the simulated rails are switched off in production (`ALLOW_SIMULATED_INTEGRATIONS`). | Each bank's switch and core system has its own specification and credentials; building against a guess would be a fake integration. | Build the adapter when the bank's specification is in hand. | The bank's technology team. |
| 9 | **Platform administrator** sees administration only (tenants, users, rule packs, audit trail, enquiries); no tenant financial data. | BRD section 3. | `accounts/investor_scope.py`. | Product owner. |
| 10 | **Release requires four different people** (maker, independent checker, Shariah reviewer, authorized signatory); a related-party transaction needs a different person to give the elevated approval than the reviewer; a daily close, a disclosure and a freeze lift are signed or approved by someone other than the preparer. | BRD segregation of duties; enforced by the system, not by procedure. | Approval matrix limits are configuration; the separation itself is code. | Risk & Compliance. |
| 11 | **Backups**: a daily logical dump with a checksum and a restore check (`backend/scripts`). The BRD's RPO of 15 minutes needs continuous WAL archiving on the database server. | A script cannot give a 15-minute recovery point; the database service must. | Configure pgBackRest / Barman or the cloud provider's point-in-time recovery. | Infrastructure team. |
| 12 | **Allocation capacity**: after the profiling fix the engine processes roughly 10,000 to 13,000 balance rows per second per process (measured at 500 and 5,000 accounts over 30 days) (see `docs/DEPLOYMENT.md`). A larger pool is run in partitions (by account range) across workers. | Measured, not assumed. | `manage.py benchmark_allocation`. | Infrastructure team, with your real volumes. |

## What these decisions do not replace
Nothing here is a fatwa, a tax opinion or legal advice (the BRD says the same of itself). Items 1, 2, 3 and 7 must be confirmed
by the named professionals before the platform handles real customer money.
